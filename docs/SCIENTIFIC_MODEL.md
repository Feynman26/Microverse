# Scientific Model

## 1. Modeling position

Microverse is a mechanistic artificial-life model, not a quantitative whole-cell model of a named organism. Biological concepts are compressed into a tractable digital chemistry while retaining causal structure:

`environment -> transport -> intracellular chemistry -> energetic state -> damage/repair -> biomass -> division -> heredity`

The model is useful only if its abstractions are explicit. A parameter should never acquire an implied real-world unit or interpretation that it was not calibrated to support.

## 2. Initial units and dimensional discipline

M0-M2 use:

- distance: virtual micrometres (`um` in names);
- time: virtual minutes;
- extracellular chemicals: internally consistent concentration/amount units;
- ATP, precursor, ROS and damage: dimensionless coarse-grained intracellular pools.

The first milestone therefore supports qualitative and comparative experiments, not prediction of real bacterial doubling times or metabolite concentrations.

A later calibration milestone may map selected parameters onto an E. coli-like or minimal-cell reference, but calibration must not change the causal architecture.

## 3. Chamber

Default chamber:

- 64 x 64 lattice sites;
- 1 virtual um per lattice spacing;
- closed/no-flux boundaries;
- stationary cells during M0-M5;
- glucose and oxygen fields in M0-M2.

The closed chamber is intentional: the first validation should demonstrate depletion, growth, division and eventual resource limitation without external forcing. Chemostat and microfluidic boundary conditions are later experiment modes.

## 4. Diffusion

For each extracellular compound `C`:

`dC/dt = D * Laplacian(C) + sources - sinks`

M0 computes the diffusion-only component with an explicit five-point stencil. Cell uptake/release is handled in separate phases.

No-flux boundaries are implemented by substituting the boundary site's own concentration for a missing outside neighbour. In a field with no cellular source/sink, total amount must remain constant within floating-point tolerance.

### Time-scale warning

Real small-molecule diffusion is much faster than bacterial growth. The M0 coefficients are deliberately compressed to keep the transparent explicit solver stable. They must not be interpreted as calibrated physical diffusion coefficients. Before quantitative biological claims, diffusion and growth time scales must be separated by substepping or a quasi-steady solver.

## 5. Membrane transport

For external substrate `S`, the ancestral M0-M2 request follows saturable transport:

`v_transport = Vmax * transport_scale * S_ext / (Km + S_ext)`

The per-tick request is capped by an intracellular pool capacity proportional to cell volume.

When multiple cells occupy the same lattice site, they compute requests against the same pre-consumption concentration. If total demand exceeds supply, all requests are multiplied by the same local scaling factor. This avoids an implementation-order advantage.

M3+ replaces `transport_scale` with actual transporter abundance and evolved affinity.

## 6. Compressed respiration

M0-M2 use one coarse-grained reaction:

`glucose + 2 oxygen -> ATP + precursor + ROS`

This is not stoichiometrically intended to reproduce biochemical respiration. It represents the causal coupling between carbon uptake, electron-acceptor availability, energetic yield, biomass precursor generation and oxidative cost.

Flux is limited by:

- intracellular glucose;
- intracellular oxygen;
- catalytic capacity proportional to cell volume and ancestral respiration scale.

Products:

- ATP supports maintenance, repair, growth and division;
- precursor supports biomass growth;
- ROS produces a cost of oxidative metabolism.

M4/M7 replace this single reaction with a reaction catalog containing alternative pathways and evolvable enzyme affinity.

## 7. ATP and energetic accounting

ATP is an abstract free-energy currency rather than conserved material. It is generated by metabolism and spent explicitly.

Current sinks:

- maintenance;
- repair;
- biomass synthesis;
- division.

Future sinks:

- transcription/translation;
- active transport;
- motility;
- secretion;
- DNA repair;
- membrane maintenance.

There must be no positive biological action that is free unless deliberately justified.

## 8. Maintenance and energetic failure

Maintenance demand is:

`maintenance = maintenance_rate * volume * dt`

If ATP is insufficient, unpaid demand accumulates as `energy_debt`. Energy debt can decay slowly after maintenance is fully paid, representing recovery from reversible energetic stress.

When debt exceeds a lethal threshold the cell dies from `energy_failure`.

This avoids the unrealistic rule `ATP == 0 -> instant death` while retaining a bounded survival period during starvation.

## 9. ROS, damage and repair

Respiration generates ROS. ROS decays spontaneously and produces damage:

`damage_rate ~ ROS`

Basal repair removes damage at an ATP cost. Death occurs if damage exceeds a lethal threshold.

This creates a first genuine trade-off:

- faster oxidative metabolism can provide more ATP and precursor;
- the same flux increases ROS burden;
- repair improves survival but consumes ATP.

M3+ should allow repair investment and antioxidant mechanisms to evolve rather than remain fixed scales.

## 10. Biomass and growth

A coarse-grained precursor pool is generated by metabolism. Growth requires both precursor and ATP.

Growth flux is limited by:

- available precursor;
- ATP available for biosynthesis;
- a kinetic ceiling proportional to cell volume.

Cell volume increases according to consumed growth precursor.

This makes growth a consequence of chemical and energetic state rather than a directly assigned rate.

## 11. Cell division

A cell becomes division-competent when:

- volume exceeds the division threshold;
- enough ATP exists to pay division cost.

At division:

1. the parent pays the explicit division ATP cost;
2. the parent lifecycle ends;
3. two new immutable cell IDs are created;
4. daughter generation is parent generation + 1;
5. volume and intracellular pools are partitioned with a small seeded stochastic asymmetry;
6. daughter positions receive small opposite seeded offsets;
7. parent/daughter relation is logged.

M0-M2 do not yet model DNA replication explicitly. M3 introduces genome replication and mutations; a later cell-cycle refinement may require completed replication before division.

## 12. Death and lysis

M0-M2 return only transportable glucose and oxygen pools to the extracellular fields when a cell dies physiologically. Other intracellular quantities are treated as dissipated/unmodelled.

This is intentionally temporary. Once extracellular carbon intermediates, amino acids, nucleotides and lipids exist, lysis should release a mass-balanced subset of those pools, allowing scavenging and potentially emergent predatory/necrotrophic strategies.

## 13. What must become evolvable

The fixed scales currently present on `CellState` are scaffolding:

- glucose transport scale;
- oxygen transport scale;
- respiration scale;
- growth scale;
- repair scale.

They must be removed as direct phenotype knobs as the genome/proteome system comes online.

The target causal route is:

`gene sequence/regulation -> mRNA -> protein abundance/properties -> reaction/transport flux -> physiology`

A mutation should usually alter molecular mechanism first; physiological consequences should be downstream.

## 14. Minimal digital chemistry target

A useful M7 chemistry should contain roughly 15-30 primitive species. Candidate groups:

### Environmental resources

- carbon source G;
- oxygen O2;
- reduced nitrogen N;
- phosphorus P.

### Energetic/redox currencies

- ATP/ADP;
- reduced/oxidized carrier pair.

### Carbon intermediates

- several abstract carbon skeletons with different redox/size signatures.

### Biomass precursors

- amino-acid pool;
- lipid precursor;
- nucleotide precursor.

### Ecological compounds

- at least two diffusible waste/intermediate compounds;
- one oxidative/toxic compound;
- one initially neutral diffusible compound capable of acquiring signalling relevance through evolved sensing.

The chemistry catalog should permit multiple routes between useful precursors, including at least one low-yield route that remains viable without oxygen. Otherwise oxygen limitation has only one outcome: death.

## 15. Evolvable catalytic specificity

A protein should not be permanently named after one reaction. The target system represents protein catalytic features and reaction motifs with digital signatures.

Affinity can initially be a monotonic function of Hamming-like distance:

`affinity = exp(-k * distance(protein_signature, reaction_signature))`

Important consequences:

- a protein may weakly catalyse multiple reactions;
- point mutation can alter several activities pleiotropically;
- duplication can preserve one copy while another diverges;
- a previously negligible side activity can become ecologically useful.

Before adoption, the signature model must be tested for evolvability. If random mutations almost never connect useful functions, or if every protein promiscuously catalyses everything, the landscape is unsuitable.

## 16. Regulation target

Genes will contain regulatory binding motifs. Regulatory proteins bind according to signature affinity and act as activators or repressors.

Expression should include:

- basal promoter strength;
- activator occupancy;
- repressor occupancy;
- explicit transcription cost;
- mRNA degradation;
- translation cost;
- protein degradation.

Noise is introduced through the seeded RNG. Noise amplitude may later evolve through promoter architecture/stability rather than a direct `noise_trait`.

No special-purpose `dormancy` regulator exists. Dormancy-like states should arise when regulation reduces expensive biosynthetic processes enough to lower energetic demand.

## 17. Mutation target

M3 begins with point-like parameter/signature mutations and regulatory changes. Structural mutation follows only after deterministic heredity is verified.

Target operators:

- promoter mutation;
- regulatory motif mutation;
- coding-signature mutation;
- gene duplication;
- gene deletion;
- inversion/rearrangement;
- later mobile-element insertion/removal.

Mutation rate should eventually depend on genome replication/repair machinery. Lower error rates must cost resources so high fidelity is not unconditionally optimal.

## 18. Ecology target

Ecological outcomes are consequences of cell chemistry and space.

### Competition can arise through

- depletion of shared substrate;
- occupation of physical space;
- oxygen depletion;
- waste accumulation;
- damaging secreted chemistry;
- interception of another lineage's extracellular products.

### Positive interaction can arise through

- waste consumption;
- detoxification;
- extracellular resource processing;
- leakage/lysis products;
- environmental modification.

### Stable diversification requires

The model must offer at least two persistently exploitable niches. A world with one perfectly substitutable substrate and one optimal reaction pathway will tend toward optimization/exclusion rather than sustained ecological diversification.

## 19. Spatial structure

Space is not cosmetic. Local diffusion creates interaction range. Clonal reproduction keeps relatives locally associated once physical positioning is introduced. This can fundamentally alter whether extracellular benefits favour producers, relatives, unrelated competitors or cheaters.

M6 therefore adds explicit particle mechanics before public-good/secretion experiments are interpreted.

## 20. Experiment philosophy

Claims about an evolved mechanism require intervention when possible.

Example workflow for a candidate innovation:

1. detect expansion of lineage L;
2. identify mutations relative to its closest non-expanding sister;
3. freeze/fork the pre-expansion state;
4. revert one mutation in an otherwise identical clone;
5. run replicated competition under the original environment;
6. compare division rate, survival, fluxes and descendants;
7. test the same pair under at least one alternate environment.

This distinguishes correlation from causal contribution and exposes genotype-by-environment dependence.

## 21. Validation hierarchy

### Numerical

- nonnegative concentrations;
- diffusion convergence and conservation;
- update-order fairness;
- reproducibility;
- snapshot/replay identity.

### Mechanistic

- no growth without usable energy source;
- resource uptake saturates;
- ATP costs reduce growth when increased;
- respiration requires both substrates;
- ROS burden rises with respiratory flux;
- division partitions pools and records genealogy.

### Evolutionary

- neutral variants show drift rather than deterministic advantage;
- deliberately introduced costly/beneficial mechanistic perturbations behave directionally in controlled conditions;
- removing environmental differences removes corresponding selective differences;
- same genotype can rank differently across environments when genuine trade-offs exist.

### Emergence

Emergence is not validated by seeing a surprising animation. A claimed emergent phenomenon must be persistent, reproducible from a saved state/seed and mechanistically explainable through the model's lower-level variables.
