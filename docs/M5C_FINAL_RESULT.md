# M5-C final result: regulatory reproductive-effect hypothesis not confirmed

## Decision

M5-C does **not** pass its originally intended reproductive-selection exit claim. Generic regulation, sensing, expression cost, finite proteome, and shared translation capacity are mechanistically validated, but the attempted controlled reproductive advantage was not independently reproducible.

The project therefore retains M5-C as a falsification and moves forward. No additional circuit tuning, threshold adjustment, seed search, or third confirmation panel is authorized for M5.

## What is validated

The M5 engine now supports and tests:

- explicit stochastic mRNA/protein dynamics driven only by the universe RNG;
- physical protein cohorts retaining historical sequence across DNA mutation;
- catalytic flux from realized protein abundance;
- mutable promoter binding motifs;
- generic activator/repressor occupancy from molecular affinity;
- generic ligand/allosteric modulation, including environment-conditioned molecular responses;
- finite proteome capacity shared by all proteins;
- finite shared translation throughput;
- explicit ATP, nucleotide, and amino-acid expression costs;
- simultaneous/order-independent allocation under shared molecular scarcity;
- regulatory mutation changing molecular dynamics before any ecological interpretation;
- controlled O2-conditioned transcription changes in R03/R09 and in a deliberately neutral expression-burden construct.

These are capability claims and are supported by passing headless gates.

## What was falsified

Several M5-C architectures were tested rather than accepting the first favorable-looking result.

### Endogenous oxidative/ROS circuits

R03/R09 molecular regulation worked as designed, but reproductive panels showed weak or null genotype-by-environment interaction. In the final ROS panel both architectures reproduced in all tested stable and fluctuating runs and the interaction was exactly zero.

### Neutral expression-burden capability assay

A final construct isolated the opportunity cost of unnecessary expression. Both architectures had the same coding sequences and ordinary biochemical costs; only the responsive architecture could repress three neutral, non-catalytic burden loci in anoxia.

The first frozen panel (`24001..24024`) was encouraging but failed its predeclared normal-theory criterion:

- mean normalized differential: `+0.102924`;
- median normalized differential: approximately `+0.170776`;
- positive seeds: `17/24`;
- mean stable-high native-rate advantage: `-0.00061652`;
- one-sample t: `1.592926`, below the frozen `2.07` threshold.

Because deaths/non-divisions generated censored and strongly non-Gaussian first-division outcomes, that failed panel was not retroactively reclassified.

A second independent robust panel (`25001..25024`) was frozen before observation. It failed decisively:

- median normalized differential: `-0.096007`;
- positive seeds: `8/24`;
- stable paired controls valid: `18/24`;
- fluctuating ordinary divisions: `122/192` trajectories;
- mean stable-high native-rate advantage: `+0.00012381`.

The arithmetic mean normalized differential was `+25,665,273.684331`, but this value is scientifically meaningless: two seeds divided by an almost-zero baseline reference rate and produced normalized magnitudes on the order of `10^9`. This exposed a second problem with the assay observable itself.

## Why the M5 reproductive gate is closed rather than tuned again

The threshold `17/24` was mathematically attainable: under a 50/50 sign null, `P(X >= 17) ~= 0.03196` one-sided, and the first independent panel actually reached 17 positive seeds. The issue is not logical impossibility. The issue is lack of independent reproducibility under the current architecture and observable.

Continuing to modify circuits after seeing two confirmation panels would turn a predeclared hypothesis test into result-seeking. Microverse instead records the negative result.

## Consequence for the roadmap

M5 closes as a **mechanistic engine milestone with a failed reproductive-selection hypothesis**, not as a positive selection result.

A future population-level regulatory-selection experiment belongs in M8, after M6/M7 provide spatial mechanics and richer ecological context. The replacement design must avoid unstable division-time ratios. Preferred endpoints include fixed-horizon descendant counts, survival/extinction probability, lineage expansion, and paired competition frequencies, with seeds and effect criteria frozen before observation.

This distinction is important: failure to demonstrate selection in M5 does not invalidate the molecular regulatory machinery, and validated machinery does not imply that selection has already been demonstrated.
