# M7-C one-way cross-feeding causal assay

M7-A established extracellular molecular inventory and conservative lysis. M7-B established reversible, sequence-derived secondary membrane transport. M7-C asks whether those mechanisms plus the existing intracellular reaction network are already sufficient for one lineage's metabolic waste to become another lineage's usable substrate.

This is a controlled capability experiment, not a claim that cross-feeding has evolved spontaneously.

## Molecular chain under test

The assay uses two pre-existing reactions:

- `R04`: `C3 + NADH + ADP -> W1 + NAD + ATP`
- `R05`: `W1 + NAD + ADP -> C2 + CO2 + NADH + ATP`

A hand-constructed donor has realized R04 catalytic protein plus a W1-compatible membrane protein. A hand-constructed recipient has realized R05 catalytic protein plus a W1-compatible membrane protein. The authoritative state contains no donor, recipient, producer, consumer, cross-feeder, cooperation, or benefit flag.

The required causal chain is:

`donor C3 chemistry -> intracellular W1 -> gradient-driven W1 export -> extracellular W1 -> diffusion / local concentration -> gradient-driven recipient W1 import -> recipient R05 -> C2 + ATP`

Every arrow is an already modeled molecular or spatial operation.

## Same-site mechanistic assay

The first assay places two finite nonoverlapping cells such that both sample the same lattice site. This deliberately removes diffusion as a confounder while testing molecular handoff.

1. Donor R04 produces W1 from explicit intracellular substrates.
2. First membrane phase exports W1 from donor. Because M7-B solves from a common snapshot, recipient exchange remains exactly zero in this phase.
3. Extracellular W1 now exists in the chamber.
4. A second membrane phase imports W1 into the recipient.
5. Recipient R05 consumes imported W1 and produces explicit C2 and ATP.

W1 must be conserved across donor + recipient + extracellular field during the two transport phases.

## Counterfactual controls

### Recipient without donor

An otherwise identical recipient with R05 and W1 transport machinery receives no W1 when no donor-derived extracellular pool exists. R05 remains at zero flux and no W1-derived C2 or ATP is gained.

### Donor without W1 transport

An otherwise functional R04 donor produces intracellular W1 but cannot place it into the environment when its realized proteome lacks W1 transport activity. The recipient therefore receives no W1 and R05 remains inactive. This separates metabolic production from membrane transfer causally.

## Spatial-distance assay

A second controlled assay restores ordinary W1 diffusion. A donor exports W1 at one location, the field diffuses for a fixed number of steps, and otherwise identical recipients are placed at near and far positions.

Acceptance requires:

- measurable import at the near recipient;
- lower import at the far recipient;
- the near/far difference to correspond to the measured local W1 field concentration.

There is no distance multiplier, neighborhood bonus, or interaction radius in the biology. Distance matters only because diffusion creates different local molecular concentrations.

## Reproducibility

The complete controlled chain is repeated from identical state and seed. Donor metabolic flux, first transport ledger, second transport ledger, recipient recovery flux, and final simulation checksum must match exactly.

## Interpretation boundary

Passing M7-C demonstrates **one-way metabolic cross-feeding capability**: material generated by one cell can become a chemically useful intracellular substrate in another cell through generic chemistry, membrane physics, and space.

It does not establish:

- evolved stable coexistence;
- reciprocal cross-feeding;
- recipient population growth on donor waste alone;
- mutualism;
- cooperation or cheating;
- long-run evolutionary accessibility of the full multi-mutation genotype combination.

Those are separate controlled or evolutionary experiments. In particular, the broader M7 exit gate still requires reciprocal exchange, extracellular public-good chemistry/exploitation, detoxification, damaging chemistry/resistance, dependence controls, and any remaining genome-structure mechanisms required by the roadmap.
